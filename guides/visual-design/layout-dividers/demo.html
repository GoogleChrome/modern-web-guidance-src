Layout dividers

Each divider is chosen by what the line means and how the layout behaves: <hr> and <table> for semantics, borders or a 1px gap for flush items, and gap decorations (column-rule / row-rule) on spaced layouts. All text is from the 1818 first edition of Mary Shelley's Frankenstein.

Your browser doesn't support row-rule on flex and grid yet, so you'll see plain gaps. Content works without them.

1. Semantic break: <hr>

A thematic break between topics is content, not decoration. <hr> exposes a separator to assistive technology; CSS rules do not, so they never replace it. Every other divider below is purely visual.

3. Flush items: sibling border or gap: 1px

With no gap between items, a border on adjacent siblings is enough for a simple non-wrapping stack. In a grid of opaque cells, a 1px gap over a contrasting container background draws the lines without touching the items.

4. Multi-column: column-rule

The rule sits in the existing column gap, flush with the column edges.

It was on a dreary night of November, that I beheld the accomplishment of my toils. With an anxiety that almost amounted to agony, I collected the instruments of life around me, that I might infuse a spark of being into the lifeless thing that lay at my feet.

It was already one in the morning; the rain pattered dismally against the panes, and my candle was nearly burnt out, when, by the glimmer of the half-extinguished light, I saw the dull yellow eye of the creature open; it breathed hard, and a convulsive motion agitated its limbs.

How can I describe my emotions at this catastrophe, or how delineate the wretch whom with such infinite pains and care I had endeavoured to form? His limbs were in proportion, and I had selected his features as beautiful. Beautiful!—Great God!

Separate tiles: rules stop at the crossings and are pulled back from them, while the outer ends stay flush with the container edges.

10. Sparse grid: rule-visibility-items: between + overlap-join

No rules point toward the empty last cell, and overlap-join makes the segments meet neatly at the corners this leaves.

12. Visual hierarchy: value lists and repeat()

First: repeat(auto, …) fills gaps between fixed first and last column rules; row gaps use a double rule first, then solid. Second: repeat(2, …) repeats a rule for the first two column gaps before falling back to a dashed third.

15. Reveal on interaction

The rules are invisible at rest and fade in faintly while the container is hovered or has focus inside it. The links keep their own hover and focus styles, so the active item stays dominant, and the fade is skipped when the user prefers reduced motion.

17. Fallback patterns

First: single-column stack using a border on adjacent siblings when unsupported, with the gap halved so spacing matches. Second: fixed-track grid drawing lines with container background gradients when unsupported, without affecting layout.
